04 / HOW IT WORKS

From image to insight.

Choose the path that fits the evidence you have. Each analysis happens locally on your computer.

01 — 04
▧
STEP 01

Bring your imagery

Upload an RGB aerial image. Add a matching binary reference mask if you want to measure overlap; white pixels represent the target class.

◈
STEP 02

Choose a method

Explore excess green, evaluate an existing prediction mask, or train a random forest from a labeled image and apply it to a separate image.

◫
STEP 03

Inspect the result

Switch between the original, overlay, prediction, errors and coverage grid. With a reference mask, compare IoU, Dice, precision and recall.

↗
STEP 04

Export & review

Save the binary mask, a JSON report or grid CSV. Verify results against independent field data before drawing conclusions.

What does the demo show?

The sample is computer-generated, not a real field. The green threshold highlights vegetation-like color; it does not distinguish cultivated rice from weedy rice. The coverage grid is in image coordinates, not a geographic map.

05 / RESEARCH NOTES

A protocol, not a leaderboard.

The paper proposes an evidence chain for an eventual comparison. Its numerical model comparison is illustrative, not measured.

PAPER BRIEF ↗
01 / DATA INTEGRITY

Protect the split.

Group overlapping physical footprints before cropping. Keep related views in the same train, validation or test partition to reduce spatial leakage.

02 / MODEL COMPARISON

Keep controls consistent.

Compare U-Net R50, DeepLabV3+ R50 and SegFormer-B0 using the same data, split, crop size, training budget and evaluation rules.

03 / SPATIAL VALIDATION

Check the geometry.

Pixel overlap and geographic accuracy are different questions. Coverage maps need registration, a valid-area rule and independent checks.
